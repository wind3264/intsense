Substitute $x = \tan\theta$; the $\frac{dx}{1 + x^2}$ becomes $d\theta$:

$$
I = \int_0^{\pi/4} \ln(1 + \tan\theta) \, d\theta.
$$

Now apply the King property $\theta \to \frac{\pi}{4} - \theta$.
Since $1 + \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{4} - \theta\right) = \frac{2}{1 + \tan\theta}$,

$$
I = \int_0^{\pi/4} \big(\ln 2 - \ln(1 + \tan\theta)\big) \, d\theta = \frac{\pi}{4}\ln 2 - I,
$$

so $I = \frac{\pi \ln 2}{8}$.
