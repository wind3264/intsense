By the King property, $I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \ln(\sin x) \, dx = \int_0^{\pi/2} \ln(\cos x) \, dx$.
Adding the two forms,

$$
2I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \ln\frac{\sin 2x}{2} \, dx = \int_0^{\pi/2} \ln(\sin 2x) \, dx - \frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2.
$$

With $u = 2x$ the remaining integral is $\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi} \ln(\sin u) \, du$, and by symmetry about $\frac{\pi}{2}$ that equals $I$.
So $2I = I - \frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2$, giving $I = -\frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2$.
